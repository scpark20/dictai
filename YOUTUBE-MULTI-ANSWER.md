# YouTube dictation: multiple accepted answers

## Scope and evidence

The implementation preserves subtitle wording and video audio. It changes only the English YouTube answer matcher, numeric token boundaries, and the input handling needed to enter multiword alternatives. It does not regenerate speech, change the speech-recognition model, alter Book/Conversation matching, or repair supposedly incorrect captions.

A public-caption audit retrieved **19 videos, 4,686 grouped practice clips, and 89,182 whitespace-delimited words**. Fifteen selected tracks were marked non-auto and four auto-generated by the caption provider. These labels do not establish transcription accuracy. The corpus includes statistics talks, mathematics explanations, a programming course, business and entertainment videos, study advice, and spoken presentations. It is a purposive English sample, not a representative sample of all YouTube dialects or genres.

The full available text of each retrieved video was scanned automatically for numeric and orthographic candidates. Short timestamped examples were inspected to guide rules. This was not a human line-by-line or audio-alignment audit. Only metadata, counts and short evidence fragments are retained; full transcripts are not redistributed. Each video's retained evidence is capped at 24 words.

One of the initial 20 videos was unavailable. A follow-up six-video set covering cooking, physics and BBC learning material returned `IpBlocked`. Those six were **not read** and do not contribute to the reported corpus. No cookies, proxies, credentials, alternative IP routes or access bypasses were used. Public transcript ingestion can be temporarily unavailable even when original-video playback still works.

## Product policy

A permissive speech-evaluation normalizer is not automatically a safe dictation answer key. Whisper's public English normalizer combines number/currency handling with contraction, title and spelling transformations; some transformations are deliberately broader than this product needs. For example, unrestricted possessive/contraction expansion or “St.” expansion can change meaning. Its implementation is a useful reference for classes to investigate, not code copied into DictAI. [Whisper English normalizer](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/normalizers/english.py).

NeMo's text-processing project separately treats text normalization and inverse normalization. That distinction supports keeping display text separate from matching representations. No NeMo runtime, model, dependency upgrade, GPU task or external semantic judge was introduced. [NVIDIA NeMo text processing](https://github.com/NVIDIA/NeMo-text-processing).

Three rules govern matching:

1. **Display the source form.** `$25`, `3.5`, `3:30`, `25%` and `Mr.` remain visible as those forms when revealed. Existing Unicode canonicalization is retained; ordinary sentence punctuation is still outside the word-slot UI.
2. **Accept bounded, explicit alternatives.** Matching compares deterministic caption-local alternatives, not embedding similarity, edit distance, homophone similarity or an LLM's interpretation.
3. **Preserve distinctions.** Cardinal/ordinal, numeric value, sign, currency symbol and unit must not disappear merely to obtain a match. Ambiguous forms are not globally expanded.

| Explicit units | 5kg ↔ five kilograms; 10km ↔ ten kilometres | No unit conversion; kg does not equal km |
| Numeric compounds | 25-year-old ↔ twenty-five year old | Missing the compound's words does not complete its slot |
| Titles | Mr. ↔ mister; Mrs. ↔ missus; Dr. ↔ doctor | St. is not guessed to mean saint or street |
| Listed initialisms | HIV ↔ h i v; U.S. ↔ u s; TV ↔ t v | No acronym-to-full-name semantic paraphrase |
| Unambiguous contractions | didn't ↔ did not; can't ↔ cannot / can not; we're ↔ we are | he's and she'd are not blindly expanded |
| Orthography | e-mail ↔ email / e mail; OK ↔ okay | Not a general synonym or concatenation rule |
| Leading-zero codes | 007 ↔ zero zero seven / oh oh seven | 007 is not 7 |

Currency names such as “pounds”, slash forms such as 3/4, and clock-shaped strings still carry contextual ambiguity in natural language. Accepted readings are explicit orthographic/readout alternatives; this implementation does not claim semantic disambiguation from audio. Ambiguous slash **dates** are not converted to named dates. Relative clock readings are bounded to clock-shaped values; no elapsed-duration conversion is provided.

## Matcher and interaction design

The previous tokenizer discarded dollar/percent signs and split decimals and colon times into unrelated word slots. A dedicated tokenizer now keeps formatted quantities atomic. Unsupported scientific notation, versions, date strings, ranges and numeric compounds remain opaque rather than silently losing digits or separators.

Targets may span multiple original word slots. Consequently numeric input “25” can solve the original “twenty five”, while “twenty five dollars” can solve one original $25 slot. Solved and revealed statuses remain distinct. Repeated values require separate submissions: answering the first $10 does not open every $10 in the caption.

Numeric input is treated as a whole expression before partial fallback. A failed “seventy one” must not open 70 by matching only “seventy”. Boundary checks also apply when ordinary words precede a quantity, so “I paid seventy one dollars” cannot sneak through a target “I paid seventy dollars”.

Space normally retains its existing submit behavior. If the text is a prefix of a known multiword alternative, it stays in the input with a trailing space and a short continuation message. Enter explicitly submits the current text. This is essential for twenty → twenty five → twenty five dollars: clearing “twenty” after the first Space would make the alternative unusable. Mixed submissions preserve unmatched or unfinished text instead of discarding it after another word matches. Duplicate-only input stays untouched.

## Progress and Names compatibility

The progress key remains unchanged, preserving video/language/transcript identity and sentence location. Saved records now include tokenizer version 2. Older opened-slot arrays migrate by character overlap against the legacy tokenizer. A new numeric slot inherits a solved state only if every old token it covers was already opened; any revealed part remains revealed. This avoids falsely completing a decimal when only one digit component was solved.

The existing Names endpoint returns indices in the old tokenizer's coordinate system. The frontend translates these through character spans into the new slot layout. A decimal before Mr. Green therefore cannot shift the reveal action onto the wrong words. The typed draft remains independent of Names.

No production progress database rewrite is part of this deployment. Browser-local records migrate only when the corresponding transcript is loaded.

## Validation and limits

The regression suite covers accepted alternatives, deliberately rejected near misses, numeric boundaries after ordinary words, repeated occurrences, mixed submissions, character-span migration, and non-English-track isolation. Property checks sample cardinal values from 0 through 999 and ensure that each next integer is rejected. Mocked DOM tests exercise the actual Space/Enter handlers, display of original symbols, duplicate-input retention, completion controls, progress migration, and Names after a decimal.

These are deterministic unit and mocked interaction tests, not evidence of real YouTube playback, pronunciation matching or perfect interpretation of captions. English equivalences are disabled for Korean tracks. No new ASR or audio changes are included.

A 38-slot fixture measured approximately 9.3 ms for the first matcher construction and 0.01 ms per cached submission on the development machine. This is a local microbenchmark, not a device-wide performance guarantee. A bounded cache avoids rebuilding the same caption rules on each keystroke.

Deliberate limits include ambiguous named dates, ordinal/cardinal interchange, arbitrary acronym expansion, Roman-numeral interpretation, scientific expressions, version numbers, unit conversion, uncertain contractions, and automatic repair of wrong captions. Long or unusual numeric constructions beyond the bounded matcher may require entering the source form. Numeric word groups with unclear list-vs-number structure are treated conservatively rather than guessed. These limits should be extended only with an explicit acceptance rule and paired rejection tests.
